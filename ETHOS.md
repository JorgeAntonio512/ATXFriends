# What I Ask of You

**Read this first: nothing on this page is legally binding.**

The legal terms for this code are in [LICENSE](./LICENSE) — the GNU Affero
General Public License v3.0, plus one exception for app store distribution.
That's the part with teeth.

This page is different. These are requests, founder to builder. I'm writing
them down because the reason this project is open source at all is the
mission, and a license can't carry a mission. Only people can.

---

## 1. Keep it local

If you take this code and launch it somewhere else, please make it **yours**,
and please make it **one city**.

Rebrand it. If you're in Boston, make it Boston Buddies. Portland, Portland
Pals. Wherever you are, name it after where you are.

This isn't territorial. It's how the thing actually works. An app that
matches people for coffee on Thursday needs *density* — enough people in one
place with overlapping schedules that a match is a real option and not a
theoretical one. A friendship app spread thin across a country is a list of
strangers you'll never meet. A friendship app concentrated in one city is a
neighborhood.

Home is where the heart is. Build for your home.

## 2. Please don't make it a subscription

We are all being nickeled to death by monthly charges, and a product meant to
address loneliness should not be one more of them. Loneliness is not a
premium tier.

I don't think "free and unsustainable" is the answer either, so here's what I'd
suggest instead: **monetize the venue, not the user.** Coffee shops, bars,
restaurants, climbing gyms, bookstores — they all want people walking through
the door on a slow Tuesday. You are in the business of sending them exactly
that. Partner with them. Let the local businesses fund the local app.

If you find a better model that keeps users out of a monthly charge, tell me
about it. I'd like to steal it.

## 3. Keep your code open

The license already requires this, so this one is belt-and-suspenders. But I
want to say the *why* out loud:

If ten cities each run their own version and each one keeps its improvements
private, we've built ten small apps. If ten cities share, we've built one good
one, ten times over. The bug you fix in Denver fixes Austin. The safety feature
you design in Chicago protects Austin.

This only works as a commons. Please treat it like one.

---

## The one test I'd hold it to

If you build a version of this, here's the question I'd ask of every feature
you ship:

> **Does this get people off the app and into a room together?**

A friendship app succeeds when it goes quiet. Six people at a taco place on a
Saturday and nobody checking their phone is the win condition. Streaks, badges,
endless matching, notification loops that pull people back in — all of that is
easy to build, all of it makes the numbers go up, and all of it quietly turns
a cure for loneliness into another cause of it.

Be a little bad at retention. On purpose.

## About the name

"ATX Friends" is mine, and I'm keeping it. That's not me being precious about
branding — it's the one honest lever I have. The license can't stop someone
from taking this code and building something that works against everything on
this page, but at least they won't be able to do it under my name, and users
won't confuse the two.

So: use the code freely, use your own name. That's the deal, and it's the same
deal I'm asking for in request #1 anyway.
